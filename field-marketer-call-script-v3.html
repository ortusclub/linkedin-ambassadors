Screening Call · ~15 min

Promo team — one-call script

One call per applicant. Explain the role, ask the six set questions in order, gauge if they're comfortable → decide the pool. Same flow every call so the recordings and scores are comparable.

You barely have to type during the call — just listen.

1 · Open 1 min

Say"Hi [Name], thanks for hopping on! This is a quick, casual chat — I'll walk you through what the role actually is, ask you a few questions, and you can ask me anything. Cool if I record it just for our notes? 👍"

2 · Explain what it is 4 min

Say — the simple version"So — think of LinkedIn as Facebook, but for professionals. In sales and marketing it's the #1 place businesses find clients. The catch is LinkedIn limits how many people you can reach from one account, so businesses hit a wall — one account only grows them so far.

That's the gap we fill. We connect businesses that want more reach with everyday people who have a LinkedIn account they're barely using — the business gets to grow, and the account owner gets paid every month for it. We call those owners our ‘ambassadors.’

Your role would be finding those ambassadors — out on the ground at Market! Market!, handing out flyers, talking to people, and helping the interested ones sign up. That's really it."
Pay — frame it as a strength"Pay is ₱2,000 a day base, for every day you work — flexible and on-call. On top of that, ₱500 for every person who successfully signs up and gets onboarded — uncapped, so the more good sign-ups you bring in, the more you earn. You can even boost it by sharing your own sign-up link online, not just flyers. Training's free."

3 · The honesty check 2–3 min

This is the key gate, and it will come up as "is this against the rules?" — so raise it yourself and be honest, don't dodge it. Honesty here builds trust and weeds out anyone who'd be uncomfortable. The truth: yes, account sharing is against LinkedIn's terms — but it's not illegal, and we do it responsibly.
Say"One thing I'll be totally straight with you about: technically, LinkedIn's terms don't allow account sharing — so it sits in a grey area, like a lot of growth and automation tools. It's not illegal, and no one gets scammed. Here's why we do it responsibly:"
  • It's always with the owner's consent. Every account is shared willingly by a real person who gets paid — nothing happens without them agreeing.
  • The renter never gets the password. We hold the login securely and set our email as the recovery contact (for any 2FA / security checks); the renter only accesses the account through our software — they never see the login. The owner can change it or pull their account back anytime.
  • We screen who rents. Accounts go to vetted, legitimate businesses for normal outreach — not scammers, nothing harmful.
  • It's kept safe. Access runs through secure software (an anti-detect browser + dedicated proxy), so it behaves like normal usage.
  • Worst case is rare and recoverable — an account could get temporarily restricted, but that's uncommon with our setup, and the owner always chose to take part.
"So it's a grey area we're upfront about — but it's consent-based, screened, and secure. That's the whole model. How do you feel about that?"
✅ Tone: calm and matter-of-fact — you're not defensive, just transparent. Comfortable / unfazed = good. Clearly uneasy or judgmental after the honest explanation = probably not a fit, and that's useful to know now.

4 · The six set questions 6 min

Ask all six, same order every call. The green tag shows which sheet column each one fills — so the transcript translates cleanly into the row.

  1. Tell me a bit about yourself — what are you up to these days? Signal: clear communicator? easy to talk to? → Communicates / confident (1–5)
  2. Have you done any promo, sales, or people-facing work before? Signal: not required — just gauge comfort. "No" is fine if they're outgoing. → Prior people-facing work (Yes/No + note)
  3. How comfortable are you walking up to strangers and starting a conversation? Signal: THE core skill. Confident vs shy. → Approaching strangers (1–5)
  4. If someone brushes you off or says no, how do you handle it? Signal: resilience — street promo gets a lot of no's. → Handles rejection (1–5)
  5. What made you interested in this role? Signal: genuine interest in the work vs. only the money. → Reliable / genuinely interested (1–5)
  6. Are you free for the trial day at Market! Market! (end of July), and onsite work generally? Signal: availability + serious commitment. → Available for trial + onsite (Yes/No)
Comfortable with the model? — you already have this from Step 3; it's the dealbreaker column, not a separate question.
Quick logistics — ask before you close

Two fast ones. They matter for scheduling field days and go into the row's Notes.

  1. Whereabouts are you based / where do you live? Signal: how close to Market! Market! / BGC — closer = easier to slot into short-notice field days.
  2. What's your general availability — any days or times that work best for you week to week? Signal: student vs. free daytime; how flexible for on-call days.
Reading the answers — green vs red flags

Green flags:

  • Curious, asks practical questions ("where would I hand out flyers?", "how do I get paid?").
  • Comfortable with the model once explained — not squeamish.
  • Sounds confident talking to strangers; maybe has done sales / promo / events before.
  • Reliable vibe — asks about schedule, takes it seriously.

Red flags:

  • Clearly uneasy or judgmental about the model even after the honest explanation.
  • Only interested in the money, no interest in the actual work.
  • Shy / uncomfortable talking to people (it's a people-facing role).
  • Flaky signals — vague on availability, low energy.

5 · Close 1 min

Say"That's everything from me — any questions? … Great, thanks [Name]! We're speaking with a few people this week and I'll be in touch about next steps, including the trial day. Appreciate your time!"

If they're a clear strong yes, you can say so and cover which areas they'd work, how flyers + their sign-up link work, and payout timing. If unsure, keep it to "we'll be in touch" and decide from the recording.
